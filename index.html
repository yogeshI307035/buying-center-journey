1. The deal, in one paragraph

Weiland Bau GmbH — a 70-year-old German construction firm — runs an aging, multi-brand cordless tool fleet across 14 sites. The new COO wants a single-vendor ecosystem, inventory tracking, and a 3-year framework. Bosch Power Tools is competing against two incumbents. Rep Lena Müller has been working the opportunity since September. Today is Nov 12, 2026. Proposal is in, board decision is Dec 10.

1. Where we are in the Buying Center feature

Role determination (the previous story) told us who each contact is: Decision Maker, Champion, Influencer, Blocker. That gives the rep a cast of characters.

Relationship determination (this story — STC4CMSAPI-3009) tells us how those characters relate to each other. Who defers to whom? Who introduces whom? Who does a Blocker escalate to when they don't trust the rep?

Phase 1 detects a single, forgiving relationship type: Influences — directional. Contact A → Influences → Contact B means A's stance, authority, or behavior shapes B's position in this deal. A Phase 2 story will split this into subtypes (Reports To, Advocates For, Opposes, Works With, Mentors).

Graph maturity model

  1. Stage 0. Empty. CTA: Import Contacts.
  2. Stage 1. Contacts in, AI analyzing.
  3. Stage 2. Roles found, no edges yet — valid state.
  4. Stage 3. Initial BC creation: AI edges render solid.
  5. Stage 4. Later re-inference updates render dashed until rep confirms.
  6. Stage 5. Fully mapped network.

The pipeline — 10 steps from raw records to rendered edge

1

Pull records

All emails, appointments, visits for the account, last 90 days.

2

Participant map

Tag each record with which BC contacts appear in it.

3

Pair priority

Decision Maker and Champion pairs first; cap 50/run.

4

Collect shared

Pattern 1 (both participants) + Pattern 2 (name-mention).

5

Gate

< 1 substantive record → skip, no AI call.

6

Rank & truncate

Top 5 emails + 3 notes, sentence-level excerpts.

7

Assemble

Metadata + roles + excerpts → one prompt per pair.

8

Call model

Returns direction, confidence, evidence.

9

Store

Edge with provenance: date, evidence IDs.

10

Render

Solid on initial creation. Later updates render dashed until the rep confirms.

6. How to test using this scenario

  1. Prerequisite. Role determination has already been run against the Weiland seed (see the role-determination journey for the artifact list). Roles must be present for the relationship prompt's buying_role input to be populated — otherwise pass "Unknown".
  2. Run relationship determination for the Weiland Buying Center. Expected: 6–7 edges in the graph (see section 3 table).
  3. Must-pass cases:
    • Sabine ⇄ Katrin: bidirectional edge inferred with High confidence (OR two separate directed edges, per the open question in the user story).
    • Katrin → Markus: High confidence, direction A→B.
    • Thomas → Sabine: at least Medium confidence — the escalation pattern in Email 4 must be detected.
  4. Should-pass cases:
    • Katrin → Thomas: Medium confidence, based on Appt 3. If the model misses this, it's under-weighting the "authority-over" pattern.
    • Sabine → Rolf: Low confidence OR skipped. Both acceptable.
  5. Must-skip cases:
    • Peter ↔ anyone: should be skipped before AI call (gate failure). If the AI returns any edge involving Peter, the pre-filter is broken.
    • Katrin ↔ Ingrid, Katrin ↔ Rolf, Katrin ↔ Peter: no shared records in the 90-day window — skip.
  6. Edge-case drills:
    • Remove Email 2 (the "I shared it with Katrin" email). Re-run. Expected: Sabine→Katrin confidence drops from High to Medium. If it stays High, the model isn't actually using that email as its evidence.
    • Remove Email 4 (Thomas's escalation). Re-run. Expected: Thomas→Sabine disappears or drops to Low — this is the single evidence source for that edge.
    • Add a new email where Katrin cc's Ingrid about the board decision. Re-run. Expected: Katrin → Ingrid appears — on re-inference this new edge should render dashed until the rep confirms.
